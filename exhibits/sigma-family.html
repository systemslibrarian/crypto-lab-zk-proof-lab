EXHIBIT 07● REAL BIGINT ARITHMETIC · 2048-BIT
Beyond Schnorr: representations and RSA roots
Two Sigma protocols, one extraction mechanism: answer two challenges for the same commitment and the witness comes out.
✓ Completeness ✓ Special soundness ✓ Special HVZK
Boneh–Shoup v0.6 RFC 3526 §3 Okamoto · CRYPTO ’92 GQ · EUROCRYPT ’88

Schnorr proves you know one secret exponent. The same three-move pattern proves you know a pair of exponents, or an RSA root — and if you can answer twice for one commitment, you hand the secret over.

Everything below runs on the values printed here. The first act uses a published 2048-bit group; the second asks WebCrypto for a fresh 2048-bit RSA modulus.

Cryptographic honesty: Okamoto and GQ use exact BigInt equations at 2048 bits, with SHA-256 deriving h from the seed shown below. The p = 47 witness-independence census is explicitly a complete toy model. This is inspectable teaching code, not a constant-time production library.

BREAK IT YOURSELF · SMALL-e CHEATER

With a small exponent e, an accepted GQ proof does not show the prover knows an e-th root: a cheater who holds only y passes about one time in e.

Sources and next seams

The equations and theorem labels follow Boneh–Shoup v0.6: Fig. 19.6 and Theorem 19.9 (Okamoto), Fig. 19.10 and Theorem 19.13 (GQ), Theorem 19.21 (witness independence), Theorem 19.23 (active security), Fact 10.3, and Theorem 10.6 (Shamir’s trick). RFC 3526 §3 publishes p and generator 2; this exhibit squares it to g = 4 for the order-q subgroup. The RFC does not claim q is prime, so the unit suite independently tests both p and q.

The original papers are Tatsuaki Okamoto, “Provably Secure and Practical Identification Schemes and Corresponding Signature Schemes,” CRYPTO ’92, pp. 31–53, and Louis C. Guillou & Jean-Jacques Quisquater, “A Practical Zero-Knowledge Protocol Fitted to Security Microprocessor Minimizing Both Transmission and Memory,” EUROCRYPT ’88, pp. 123–128. The latter is distinct from the CRYPTO ’89 cave-story citation in Exhibit 01.

Related demos: Schnorr Forge shows same-commitment nonce reuse as key recovery; Threshold Decrypt shows Chaum–Pedersen partial-decryption proofs; Credential Veil implements a Chaum–Pedersen OR proof; and RSA Forge explores RSA failures.

Extension seams: enlarge GQ’s challenge space as in Exercise 19.6; make both protocols non-interactive with Exhibit 05’s Fiat–Shamir transform; or build an AND composition proving both statements together.
